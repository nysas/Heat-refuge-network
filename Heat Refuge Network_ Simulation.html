Heat-rush test

400 people across the district look for shelter at the selected time. Without the system, each walks to the nearest refuge they know of, not knowing if it is open or full. With the system, each is sent to the best-scoring refuge that still has space, and occupancy updates as people arrive.

How the model works

Outdoor conditions

Temperature follows a daily cycle: 29°C at 3 am, peaking at 38°C (plus heatwave boost) at 3 pm. Humidity moves opposite, 75% at night to 45% in the afternoon. Local temperature adds an urban heat island: +2.2°C in the dense centre, +1.2°C near the industrial area, up to −1.8°C in the park and −2.2°C at the coast.

Heat index

“Feels like” temperature from the US National Weather Service Rothfusz regression, using temperature and humidity. Categories: under 27°C comfortable, 27–32 caution, 32–41 extreme caution, 41–54 danger, above 54 extreme danger.

Indoor conditions

Air-conditioned spaces hold 25°C but warm up once more than 60% full. Fan-cooled halls sit about 1.5°C below outdoors and warm as they fill. Shade is 3°C cooler than outside; misting shelters 3.5°C cooler with higher humidity.

Occupancy

Each refuge’s crowd grows with outdoor heat index, scaled by capacity and popularity, with small random variation. Closed refuges are empty and excluded from ranking.

Refuge Comfort Score

Score = Σ wᵢ·sᵢ / Σ wᵢ × 100 where the parts are: comfort (indoor heat index, 26°C → 1, 41°C → 0), crowding (1 when under half full, falling to 0 when full), distance (e^(−d/900 m), 500 m for vulnerable users), amenities (water, seating, toilet, first aid) and accessibility.

Assumptions

Walking distance is measured along a street grid (Manhattan distance). All data is synthetic and seeded, so the same settings always give the same results. In deployment, IoT sensors and open weather APIs replace the generated values.
